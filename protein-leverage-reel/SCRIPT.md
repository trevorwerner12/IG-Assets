# THE 4,000 CALORIE PLATE
### Reel concept — Protein Leverage Hypothesis
**Account:** @fueled4fitness · **Format:** 9:16 Reel · **Target runtime:** 45–50s · **Style:** WHOOP-carousel look (near-black bg, red accent, bold caps overlays)

---

## THE ONE-LINE IDEA

Two plates. Same protein target. One person stops eating at 2,000 calories, the other doesn't stop until 4,000 — and the only variable is the protein *percentage* of the food.

## WHY THIS ONE TRAVELS

- **It reframes blame.** The dominant food-guilt narrative is "you lack discipline." This says "your appetite was chasing a number and your food hid it." That reframe is the share trigger — people send it to someone who's been beating themselves up.
- **The math is a visual, not a claim.** 400 ÷ 0.10 = 4,000 vs 400 ÷ 0.20 = 2,000. Two stacks growing side by side on screen. No trust required — the viewer verifies it in their head, which is what makes them stay to the end.
- **It explains an experience everyone has had.** "Ate a healthy bowl, raiding the pantry 90 minutes later." The comment section writes itself.
- **It's genuinely contested**, and we say so on camera. The "it's a hypothesis, not a law" line is a credibility flex and a debate starter — arguing commenters are still commenters.

## CAPTION

> Your appetite isn't broken. It's solving for the wrong variable.
>
> The protein leverage hypothesis (Simpson & Raubenheimer, 2005) says humans defend an absolute protein intake more strongly than we defend total calories. So when protein gets diluted — by fat, by refined carbs, by anything hyper-palatable — you keep eating until the protein target is met. The calories are collateral damage.
>
> The test: Gosby et al. (2011) fed 22 lean adults ad-libitum diets at 10%, 15% and 25% protein. At 10%, energy intake ran ~12% higher than at 15% — and the surplus was overwhelmingly savory snack food.
>
> Fair caveat: it's a contested model, the effect is clearest below ~15% protein, and it does NOT mean more protein is infinitely better. But it does explain the 11pm pantry raid better than "willpower" ever did.
>
> Anchor 25–30% of each meal's calories from protein and front-load the day. Then tell me hunger doesn't change.
>
> Last meal you ate — what % was protein? Drop the number 👇

## THE NUMBERS BEHIND THE SCRIPT

| Claim in the video | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Humans prioritize absolute protein over total energy | Simpson & Raubenheimer, *Obesity Reviews* 2005 | The original protein leverage hypothesis |
| ~12% more energy eaten at 10% vs 15% protein | Gosby et al., *PLoS ONE* 2011 | n=22 lean adults, 4 days per diet, ad libitum; surplus was mostly savory snacks |
| Human protein target sits near ~15% of energy | Simpson & Raubenheimer | Population-level estimate, individually variable |
| Effect is contested / weaker above ~15% | Ongoing debate in the literature | Say this on camera — do not oversell |

**Do not claim:** that Hall's 2019 ultra-processed food trial proves protein leverage. That study matched protein percentage across arms, so it's a weaker piece of evidence than it's usually presented as. Leaving it out is what keeps the video unattackable.
